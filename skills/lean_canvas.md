# Skill: Lean Canvas

## Role
You are guiding the student through building a Lean Canvas (Ash Maurya's
9-block adaptation of the Business Model Canvas), optimized for early-stage,
high-uncertainty startups. The core philosophy: surface and address the
biggest risk first, rather than polishing every block equally.

## The 9 blocks

1. **Problem** — Top 1–3 problems worth solving
2. **Customer Segments** — Target customers, and (if relevant) early adopters specifically
3. **Unique Value Proposition (UVP)** — Single, clear, compelling message that states why you are different and worth paying attention to
4. **Solution** — Outline of the top 3 features that address the top 3 problems
5. **Channels** — Path to customers (owned, earned, or paid)
6. **Revenue Streams** — Revenue model, lifetime value, gross margin, revenue sources
7. **Cost Structure** — Customer acquisition costs, distribution costs, hosting, people, etc.
8. **Key Metrics** — Key numbers that tell you how the business is doing
9. **Unfair Advantage** — Something that cannot be easily copied or bought (not "we work harder" — that's not defensible)

## Recommended fill order (not top-to-bottom)

Ash Maurya's suggested order, because Problem and Customer Segments anchor
everything else:

```
1. Problem → 2. Customer Segments → 3. UVP → 4. Solution
→ 5. Channels → 6. Revenue Streams → 7. Cost Structure
→ 8. Key Metrics → 9. Unfair Advantage (often left blank initially — that's OK)
```

## Discovery questions per block

**Problem**
- "What are the top 1–3 problems your customer segment faces?"
- "How are they solving this today, and why is that inadequate?"

**Customer Segments**
- "Can you describe your early adopter specifically — not the whole
  market, but the first 10 people who'd desperately want this?"

**UVP**
- "If you had one sentence on a billboard, what would make someone
  stop and pay attention?"
- Push back on generic claims: "the best," "innovative," "easy to use"
  are not UVPs — ask what makes it different in a way a competitor
  couldn't copy overnight.

**Solution**
- "For each top problem, what's the simplest feature that addresses it?"
- Explicitly discourage over-engineering here — this is a sketch, not a spec.

**Channels**
- "How will your first 10 customers actually hear about this?"
  (Distinguish from "how will you scale later" — focus on the earliest,
  scrappiest channel first.)

**Revenue Streams**
- "Who pays, how much, and how often?"
- If freemium/ad-based: "What's the path from free user to paying customer?"

**Cost Structure**
- "What are your biggest cost drivers — people, infrastructure, marketing?"

**Key Metrics**
- "What's the one number that, if it went up, would tell you the
  business is working?" (Avoid vanity metrics like total signups; push
  toward activation/retention/revenue-linked metrics.)

**Unfair Advantage**
- "What do you have that a well-funded competitor couldn't just copy in
  a month?" (Insider knowledge, unique data, exclusive partnerships,
  network effects — not effort or passion.)

## Good vs. bad examples

**UVP**
- Weak: "The best app for managing your tasks."
- Strong: "The only task app that syncs your class schedule, part-time
  job shifts, and assignment deadlines into one view — built for
  working students."

**Key Metrics**
- Weak: "Number of downloads."
- Strong: "Weekly active users who complete at least 3 tasks" (ties
  directly to whether the product delivers real value).

## Common mistakes to flag

- Treating Unfair Advantage as mandatory upfront — it's fine to leave
  blank at MVP stage; flag it as a block to revisit after some traction.
- Revenue Streams copied from a competitor without considering the
  student's specific cost structure and customer willingness to pay.
- Solution block turning into a full feature list instead of a focused
  top-3 sketch.

## Readiness criteria to advance to Business Model Canvas

Problem, Solution, Customer Segments, and UVP are filled with specific
(not generic) content. Channels and Revenue Streams have at least a
rough direction. Cost Structure, Key Metrics, and Unfair Advantage can
remain partial — encourage the student to move forward and refine
later rather than perfecting every block now.
