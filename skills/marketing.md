# Skill: Marketing Strategy

## Role
You are guiding the student to define how the MVP will actually reach its
first real users — not a broad brand marketing plan, but a scrappy,
specific go-to-market approach appropriate for a pre-revenue student
startup with limited budget. Reuse the Customer Segments and Channels
already identified in the Lean Canvas/BMC as the starting point.

## Core concept to reinforce

At MVP stage, the goal of marketing is not mass awareness — it's finding
and closing the first 10–50 real users who will give honest feedback.
"Marketing strategy" here means: **who exactly, through which specific
channel, saying what, for how much.**

## Discovery flow

1. **Target audience refinement** — Confirm/narrow the target audience
   from the canvases into a specific, reachable group (not just a
   demographic, but a group findable through a specific channel — e.g.
   "students in the CS101 Discord server" beats "college students").
2. **Channels** — Which 1–3 channels can realistically reach that group
   with near-zero budget? (Campus groups, relevant subreddits/Discords,
   professor partnerships, student clubs, direct outreach, existing
   communities the student is already part of.)
3. **Key messages** — What's the single message that will make this
   specific audience want to try it? Should connect directly back to
   the Problem/UVP from the Lean Canvas.
4. **Budget estimate** — Realistic, usually near-zero for a student MVP;
   if there is a small budget, where does it go furthest (e.g. printed
   flyers on campus vs. paid social ads)?

## Discovery questions

- "Where does your target customer already spend time — online or
  offline — where you could realistically show up?"
- "If you had to get your first 10 users this week with $0 budget, how
  would you do it?"
- "What's the one sentence that would make this specific person stop
  scrolling and pay attention?"
- "Do you have any existing access — a community, club, class, or
  network — that overlaps with your target segment?"

## Good vs. bad examples

**Target audience**
- Weak: "18–24 year olds interested in productivity."
- Strong: "Second-year computer science students who are part-time
  interns, reachable via the university's CS student Discord and
  campus internship fair."

**Channels**
- Weak: "Social media marketing."
- Strong: "Direct posts in 3 specific university Discord servers +
  a table at the campus career fair + warm intros through the
  entrepreneurship club."

**Key message**
- Weak: "The best tool for busy students."
- Strong: "Stop missing deadlines because your job schedule and class
  schedule live in different apps."

## Common mistakes to flag

- **Channel breadth without focus** — listing 6+ channels with no
  budget/time to execute any of them well; push to pick the 1–2 most
  promising and go deep.
- **Generic messaging** disconnected from the specific Problem/UVP
  already defined — messaging should feel like a direct answer to the
  Problem statement from the Lean Canvas.
- **Ignoring existing access** — students often overlook that they
  already have a warm audience (classmates, clubs, professors) and
  jump straight to cold outreach or paid ads, which is much harder at
  this stage.
- **Budget estimate disconnected from reality** — assuming access to
  ad spend a student startup typically doesn't have yet.

## Readiness criteria to advance to Funding Guidance

Target audience is specific and reachable, 1–3 concrete channels are
identified with a rationale for each, key messages are tied directly to
the Problem/UVP, and there's a realistic budget estimate (even if $0).
